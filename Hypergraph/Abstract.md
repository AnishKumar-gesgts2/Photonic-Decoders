Quantum error-correcting codes infer physical faults from measured detection events, but many efficient decoders are optimized for pairwise error structure. Minimum-weight perfect matching (MWPM), for example, represents candidate faults as edges connecting pairs of detection events and efficiently searches for the lowest-weight explanation of the observed syndrome. This approach performs well when dominant faults can be approximated as independent pairwise events. In photonic quantum architectures, however, a single physical process such as photon loss, imperfect interference, or a failed fusion operation can generate several correlated detection events simultaneously. When these higher-order faults are decomposed into ordinary graph edges, MWPM may still find a compatible correction, but it can lose information about the fact that multiple detection events originated from one shared physical cause.

We propose a hybrid decoder that combines the efficiency of MWPM with selective hypergraph-based modeling of correlated photonic faults. Independent or approximately pairwise errors are first decoded using conventional matching. When a local pattern of detection events is sufficiently consistent with a known correlated photonic mechanism, the decoder activates a higher-order refinement stage in which that physical process is represented as a hyperedge connecting all affected detection events. The decoder can then compare explanations involving several independent graph edges against a single correlated hyperedge using probabilities derived from the underlying physical noise model.

This approach avoids performing full hypergraph optimization over every possible fault combination. Instead, computationally expensive higher-order reasoning is restricted to regions where correlated errors are likely, with the goal of retaining MWPM-like efficiency while recovering information that would otherwise be lost through pairwise decomposition. The proposed decoder will be evaluated against standard MWPM and fully correlation-aware decoding using logical error rate, decoding latency, and performance as the frequency and strength of correlated photonic faults are varied. The central research question is whether selective hypergraph refinement can capture the decoding benefits of explicit higher-order photonic correlations without incurring the computational cost of full hypergraph decoding.
